Yes, if the remodel moves plumbing, touches wiring or alters walls. Up to three permits, from two agencies. The Seattle Department of Construction and Inspections (SDCI) issues the building and electrical permits. Public Health Seattle & King County issues the plumbing permit.

A like-for-like swap of a vanity, a retile or fresh paint usually needs none. The line between those two cases is where most homeowners get surprised.

## Which permits does a Seattle bathroom remodel need?

Three can apply, and each has its own agency. Plumbing goes through Public Health, wiring through SDCI's electrical group, and walls or structure through SDCI's building group.

| Work in your bathroom | Permit | Who issues it |
| :- | :- | :- |
| Install, relocate or change plumbing | Plumbing permit | Public Health Seattle & King County |
| Wiring installed, altered or extended | Electrical permit | SDCI |
| Load-bearing work, building envelope, egress, light or ventilation | Building permit | SDCI |
| Cabinets, surface finishes over existing walls, painting | Usually none | n/a |

The rows come from the agencies' own pages: [SDCI on when you need a permit](https://www.seattle.gov/sdci/permits/do-you-need-a-permit), [SDCI on electrical permits](https://www.seattle.gov/sdci/permits/permits-we-issue-%28a-z%29/electrical-permit) and [Public Health on plumbing permits](https://cd10-prod.kingcounty.gov/en/dept/dph/health-safety/environmental-health/plumbing-gas-piping/applications-and-permits), all checked November 2026.

## When is a bathroom remodel exempt from a building permit?

SDCI says minor repairs or alterations costing $6,000 or less in any six-month period usually need no building permit. The limit counts the fair market value of labor and parts, even if you do the work yourself ([SDCI, checked November 2026](https://www.seattle.gov/sdci/permits/do-you-need-a-permit)).

Three things override that number. SDCI requires a permit for work on load-bearing supports, changes to the building envelope, and work that reduces egress, light, ventilation or fire resistance. It says this applies no matter how small the project.

Add up your own scope before you assume you are under $6,000. Tile, a vanity, a toilet, a fan, and the labor to put them in: it adds up fast.

The same page says installing kitchen cabinets and putting paneling or finishes over existing walls usually need no permit. It does not list tile, flooring or replaced fixtures, so a retile alone is a call to SDCI, not an assumption. SDCI's page lists a phone line for exactly that question.

## Does a plumbing change need a permit?

Yes. Public Health requires a permit to "install, relocate, or change a plumbing or gas piping system" ([Public Health Seattle & King County, checked November 2026](https://cd10-prod.kingcounty.gov/en/dept/dph/health-safety/environmental-health/plumbing-gas-piping/applications-and-permits)). Moving a toilet, a drain or a shower valve counts.

Public Health, not SDCI, issues plumbing permits in Seattle. The same office covers Clyde Hill and unincorporated King County. Other cities in the county have their own rules.

A homeowner can apply for a homeowner's permit and do the plumbing personally. Public Health itself recommends hiring a professional plumber.

### What does the 2026 plumbing permit cost?

The permit is $137 plus $27 per fixture for permits issued under the schedule effective January 1, 2026 ([Public Health fee schedule, checked November 2026](https://cdn.kingcounty.gov/-/media/king-county/depts/dph/documents/health-safety/environmental-health/fees/plumbing-gas-piping-service-fees-through-2026.pdf)).

Older numbers still circulate. The 2025 schedule was $132 plus $26 per fixture ([Seattle Building Connections, checked November 2026](https://buildingconnections.seattle.gov/2024/12/05/changes-coming-to-plumbing-gas-permit-fees/)), and the county raised it again on January 1. If a page quotes $132, it is a year old.

Here is the arithmetic for a bathroom whose permit lists four fixtures: $137 + 4 × $27 = $245. The county's fixture list decides what counts as a fixture, so your number can differ.

## Does a bathroom remodel need an electrical permit?

Yes, when wiring changes. SDCI says you need one "any time electrical wiring is installed, altered, extended, or connected to any electrical equipment" ([SDCI electrical permit page, checked November 2026](https://www.seattle.gov/sdci/permits/permits-we-issue-%28a-z%29/electrical-permit)). A new fan circuit, a moved light or a heated floor all qualify.

Professionals need a Washington electrical contractor's license and a Seattle business license. Owners who own and occupy the home may do their own work. Many permits issue the same day online, and most need three inspections: cover, service and final.

SDCI bases electrical fees on what is installed or altered, plus a technology fee. Its page cites both 2024 and 2026 fee documents, so ask for the current estimate before you budget.

## What if you change the layout?

A layout change usually triggers more than one permit at once. A moved drain brings the plumbing permit. A relocated light or outlet brings the electrical permit. Take out a wall that carries load, and the building permit applies whatever the cost.

SDCI's Subject-to-Field-Inspection (STFI) permit may cover a smaller layout change. It lists "remodeling part of a house" among the qualifying examples, screening "takes a few days", and the permit is valid for 18 months from issue ([SDCI STFI page, checked November 2026](https://www.seattle.gov/sdci/permits/permits-we-issue-%28a-z%29/construction-permit%2D%2D%2Dsubject%2Dto%2Dfield%2Dinspection)).

Projects above the STFI limits need a regular building permit. SDCI sets building fees by project value, plus a technology fee, and publishes a yearly Fee Estimator. We do not quote a building-permit number here because we have no current figure from SDCI to cite.

## What happens if you skip the permit?

Public Health has a separate price for it. An Already Built Construction permit is $273 plus $55 per fixture, against $137 plus $27 per fixture for a normal one ([Public Health fee schedule, checked November 2026](https://cdn.kingcounty.gov/-/media/king-county/depts/dph/documents/health-safety/environmental-health/fees/plumbing-gas-piping-service-fees-through-2026.pdf)). Fees you already paid are subtracted.

That is the published fee difference, and it is only the plumbing side. The agencies' pages we read do not give a fine or a resale rule, so we make no claim about them.

## How long do permits take in Seattle?

SDCI says STFI screening takes a few days, and many electrical permits issue the same day. Plan-reviewed permits take longer. SDCI publishes no single number on the pages we read, and Public Health gives no review time either. It only says inspections are requested through its Permit Center.

Ask your contractor to put the permit steps in the schedule in writing. For the sales-tax line that sits next to these fees on an estimate, see [why your estimate has a sales tax line](/blog/sales-tax-remodeling-washington/).

:::faq
- q: Do I need a permit to replace a toilet or vanity in Seattle?
  a: A like-for-like fixture swap usually does not change the plumbing system, but Public Health requires a permit to install, relocate or change it. If the supply or drain moves, you need one. If nothing moves, ask Public Health before you start.
- q: Who issues a plumbing permit in Seattle?
  a: Public Health Seattle & King County issues and inspects plumbing permits in Seattle, Clyde Hill and unincorporated King County. SDCI issues the building and electrical permits. Other King County cities send you to their own building department.
- q: Can I pull the permit myself?
  a: Yes, as an owner. Public Health offers a homeowner's plumbing permit for work you do yourself, and SDCI lets owners who own and occupy the home do their own electrical work.
- q: Is there a dollar limit for a building permit?
  a: SDCI says minor work costing $6,000 or less in any six-month period usually needs no building permit. Load-bearing work, envelope changes and anything reducing egress, light or ventilation needs one regardless of cost.
- q: What does a plumbing permit cost in 2026?
  a: Public Health charges $137 plus $27 per fixture under the schedule effective January 1, 2026. A re-inspection is $137. Building and electrical permit fees come from SDCI and depend on the project, so use their Fee Estimator.
