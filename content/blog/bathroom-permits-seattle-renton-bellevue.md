The agency that issues your permit depends on the address, not on the work. A bathroom with the same layout needs different paperwork in Seattle, Renton and Bellevue. In unincorporated King County, a fourth set of rules applies.

This page covers four of them: Seattle, Renton, Bellevue and unincorporated King County. We read each agency's own page in November 2026. Where a page was silent, we say so instead of guessing.

## Who issues which bathroom permit in each place?

Seattle and unincorporated King County send plumbing to Public Health Seattle & King County. Renton and Bellevue issue their own. Electrical is different: Seattle, Renton and Bellevue issue their own, and unincorporated King County buys the permit from Washington L&I.

| Place | Building | Plumbing | Electrical |
| :- | :- | :- | :- |
| Seattle | SDCI | Public Health Seattle & King County | SDCI |
| Renton | City of Renton | City of Renton, separate permit | City of Renton, separate permit |
| Bellevue | City of Bellevue | City of Bellevue | City of Bellevue |
| Unincorporated King County | King County | Public Health Seattle & King County | Washington L&I (see the note below) |

Sources: [Public Health on who issues plumbing permits](https://cd10-prod.kingcounty.gov/en/dept/dph/health-safety/environmental-health/plumbing-gas-piping/applications-and-permits), [Renton's remodel page](https://rentonwa.gov/City-Services/Permit-Center/Residential-Interior-Remodels), [Bellevue's permit page](https://bellevuewa.gov/city-government/departments/development/permits/faqs/work-requiring-permits) and the [MyBuildingPermit tip sheet](https://mybuildingpermit.com/sites/default/files/TS-00_Permits%20Required%20and%20Not%20Required_FINAL_6.11.26.pdf), all checked November 2026.

The King County electrical cell rests on two pages. L&I lists the cities that issue their own electrical permits, and Seattle, Renton and Bellevue are on that list while unincorporated King County is not. For jobs outside the listed cities, L&I says to "purchase your permit from L&I" ([Washington L&I, checked November 2026](https://www.lni.wa.gov/licensing-permits/electrical/electrical-permits-fees-and-inspections/city-electrical-permits-inspections)). The MyBuildingPermit tip sheet shows the same answer for King County.

## What does Seattle require?

Seattle needs up to three permits: building and electrical from SDCI, plumbing from Public Health. SDCI says minor work costing $6,000 or less in any six-month period usually needs no building permit ([SDCI, checked November 2026](https://www.seattle.gov/sdci/permits/do-you-need-a-permit)).

We wrote the whole Seattle picture, including the 2026 plumbing fee, in [bathroom remodel permits in Seattle](/blog/seattle-bathroom-permit/). Start there if your house is inside the city limits. The estimate also carries a sales tax line at the rate of the project address, which differs between these cities: see [why your estimate has a sales tax line](/blog/sales-tax-remodeling-washington/).

## What does Renton require?

Renton wants a building permit for a residential interior remodel, bathroom included. The application needs a signed building application, construction plans and the 2021 Washington State Energy Code checklist ([City of Renton, checked November 2026](https://rentonwa.gov/City-Services/Permit-Center/Residential-Interior-Remodels)).

Plumbing, electrical and mechanical work each get their own permit. All three can be applied for in Renton's online permit portal. Renton's page lists no dollar threshold and no exemptions, so we would not assume a small bathroom needs no building permit.

Renton also requires an asbestos survey of the work area before any renovation or demolition. The city cites Puget Sound Clean Air Agency and L&I rules. Plan the survey before demolition starts, because those rules require it first.

Review is the slow part. Renton quotes 2 to 3 weeks for an initial residential review and 1 to 2 weeks for revisions. Same-day permits exist for over-the-counter work. If the house is on septic and the remodel adds a bath, King County Environmental Health must approve it, and the letter goes in with your application.

## What does Bellevue require?

Bellevue is the most specific about plumbing. Showers and bathtubs require permits. So does any new or relocated fixture that needs new water lines, drains or vents ([City of Bellevue, checked November 2026](https://bellevuewa.gov/city-government/departments/development/permits/faqs/work-requiring-permits)).

Swapping a toilet, wash basin, bidet or laundry tub for a similar fixture in the same spot does not need a permit. Replacing cabinets and countertops does not need a building permit either. Electrical work does: adding or moving circuits, or changing the service panel.

Bellevue's page says nothing about tile, flooring or fans. Call the Permit Center at +1 425-452-4898 before you assume they are exempt.

## What about unincorporated King County?

Public Health issues the plumbing permit. The rule is the same as in Seattle: you need one to install, relocate or change a plumbing or gas piping system ([Public Health, checked November 2026](https://cd10-prod.kingcounty.gov/en/dept/dph/health-safety/environmental-health/plumbing-gas-piping/applications-and-permits)).

The regional tip sheet lists fixture replacement without plumbing line changes and interior finish work among work that typically needs no permit. It adds one line that catches people: tub and shower replacement does require a permit ([MyBuildingPermit, June 2026 edition, checked November 2026](https://mybuildingpermit.com/sites/default/files/TS-00_Permits%20Required%20and%20Not%20Required_FINAL_6.11.26.pdf)).

The tip sheet says to check with the local jurisdiction if the property sits in a critical area, shoreline area or sensitive area.

## Which rules repeat across these pages?

Three points show up on more than one page.

- Moving plumbing needs a plumbing permit. Public Health says so for Seattle and unincorporated King County, Bellevue says so for relocated fixtures, and Renton requires a separate plumbing permit.
- A tub or shower replacement is not treated like a toilet swap. Bellevue and the regional tip sheet both say it needs a permit.
- Interior finish work is on the tip sheet's list of work that typically needs no permit, which covers Bellevue and King County (ask Bellevue about tile, flooring and fans first). Renton's page lists no exemptions. The tip sheet adds a warning: work still has to meet code even when no permit is needed.

:::faq
- q: Is the plumbing permit the same office in Seattle and Bellevue?
  a: No. Public Health Seattle & King County issues plumbing permits in Seattle and unincorporated King County. Bellevue issues its own. A Bellevue permit does not come from Public Health, so use the city's Permit Center for that address.
- q: Does Renton require an asbestos survey for a small bathroom?
  a: Renton's page says Puget Sound Clean Air Agency and L&I rules require a survey of the work area before any renovation or demolition. It lists no size limit. Ask the Permit Center at +1 425-430-7215 if your scope is very small.
- q: Do I need a permit to replace a toilet in Bellevue?
  a: Not for a like-for-like swap in the same location. Bellevue's page lists toilets, wash basins, bidets and laundry tubs among fixtures you can replace that way. A new fixture or a moved one needs a permit.
- q: Do I need a permit to replace a tub with a shower?
  a: Bellevue says showers and bathtubs require permits, and the regional tip sheet says tub and shower replacement does too. Check the local rule before demolition, because plumbing changes are usually involved.
